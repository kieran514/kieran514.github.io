My Story

I'm Kieran Saunders, a PhD candidate at Aston University, fueled by a fascination for how we can infer the 3D structure of our world from images. My academic path began with a BSc in Mathematics, also from Aston, which provided the analytical toolkit I now apply to the dynamic field of computer vision. For the past six years, I've been immersed in this area, specifically focusing on the challenge of estimating depth using only a single camera (monocular depth estimation).

My research delves into self-supervised learning – a powerful paradigm that allows neural networks to learn about depth without needing explicit ground truth depth data. This approach unlocks scalability and adaptability, crucial for real-world deployment.

My primary research goal is to develop depth estimation systems that are not only accurate but also robust and reliable across diverse and challenging conditions. The potential impact spans autonomous navigation, realistic virtual and augmented reality experiences, and advanced robotic perception.

Research & Publications

GRASP3R: Unposed and Uncalibrated RGB Robotic Object Grasping

Kieran Saunders, Martin Rudorfer, Luis J. Manso, George Vogiatzis

In Submission to BMVC 2025

Technical Snapshot

Problem: Robotic grasping methods often require expensive depth sensors and camera calibration, limiting real-world viability.

  • Key Innovation: An end-to-end model predicting 7-DoF grasps from uncalibrated RGB image pairs, removing the need for depth sensors.
  • Key Innovation: Jointly learns 3D scene reconstruction and grasp prediction, enabling collision checking from vision alone.
  • Impact: Sets a new SOTA for RGB-only, calibration-free grasping, achieving competitive performance on the GraspNet-1Billion benchmark.

BaseBoostDepth: Exploiting Larger Baselines For Self-supervised Monocular Depth Estimation

Kieran Saunders, Luis J. Manso, George Vogiatzis

British Machine Vision Conference (BMVC) 2024

Technical Snapshot

Problem: Self-supervised methods fail to use large baselines, missing out on strong geometric cues for depth due to challenges like pose drift.

  • Key Innovation: A curriculum learning-inspired strategy to train the network progressively on wider frame separations.
  • Key Innovation: Incremental Pose Estimation to correct drift and Error-Induced Reconstructions to improve robustness.
  • Impact: Achieved SOTA on KITTI and SYNS-patches datasets across multiple metrics without increasing test-time complexity.

Self-supervised Monocular Depth Estimation: Let's Talk About The Weather

Kieran Saunders, George Vogiatzis, Luis J. Manso

IEEE/CVF International Conference on Computer Vision (ICCV) 2023

Technical Snapshot

Problem: Models trained on clear-weather data fail in real-world adverse conditions like rain, fog, or night.

  • Key Innovation: A bi-directional, pseudo-supervision loss exploiting consistency between augmented and unaugmented views.
  • Key Innovation: "Semi-augmented warping" to isolate the depth learning task from noisy pose estimations in augmented data.
  • Impact: Significantly surpasses SOTA on adverse condition datasets (DrivingStereo, Foggy CityScape, NuScenes-Night) while maintaining performance on KITTI.

Dyna-DM: Dynamic Object-aware Self-supervised Monocular Depth Maps

Kieran Saunders, George Vogiatzis, Luis J. Manso

International Conference on Autonomous Robot Systems and Competitions (ICARSC) 2023

Technical Snapshot

Problem: Estimating motion for all potentially dynamic objects is computationally wasteful and can introduce errors if they are static.

  • Key Innovation: An efficient appearance-based filter (using Dice coefficient) to identify "truly dynamic" objects, ignoring static ones.
  • Key Innovation: Showed that disregarding small, distant objects from motion estimation improves both performance and efficiency.
  • Impact: Improved depth accuracy over prior dynamic-aware methods while reducing GPU memory usage by over 29%.
